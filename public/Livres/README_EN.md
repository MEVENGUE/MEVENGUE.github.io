## The idea

The book begins with a clearly announced thought experiment: a contemporary Einstein entrusts his intellectual traces to an assistant. Not to resurrect him. To ask what a system could actually model from texts, letters, notes and corrections - and what it could never reconstruct.

That question then widens into a collective mirror: our digital, scientific and everyday traces become data; models draw representations of the world from them. The book offers tools to distinguish what is established, what is inferred, what remains hypothetical, and what stays unknown.

## What the book refuses

- Confusing a person with the traces they leave.
- Taking a fluent answer for a proof.
- Presenting a simulation as an identity or a consciousness.
- Turning a conditional scenario into a prediction.
- Inventing results, citations or sources.

## Transparency

The book was co-written with ChatGPT. Ideas, choices and intellectual responsibility remain the author's. A convincing AI answer never constitutes, by itself, a proof. Several registry sources were examined at notice, abstract or excerpt level: that level is stated explicitly.
